# Understand The Business Before Judging The Stock

Use for fundamental company research and business-quality questions, not a mandatory detour for a PE calculation, technical signal or narrow update. The output should let a reader explain how this business works, why customers choose it, who captures the economics, and what could erode them—even without a stock quote. Quality and cheapness are separate judgments.

## Map The Material System Before Narrowing

Follow one representative customer transaction from need to choice, purchase, delivery, renewal and collected cash. Distinguish user, decision-maker and payer; product, charging unit, contract duration, discounts, refunds and service obligations. Then identify material segments or customer cohorts that break that example. A company description or revenue-segment list is not this map.

Trace the value chain, distribution, suppliers, workforce/technology/assets, capacity constraints, funding and regulation that make delivery possible. Explain which party owns the customer relationship, sets terms, bears failure risk and can withhold a critical input. Separate customer value created from value retained by this company and ultimately common shareholders.

Use the dimensions below to find material gaps, not as a fixed scored checklist. Investigate beyond the initial bull/bear thesis where a neglected dependency could change the business model. Only then compress to the decisive drivers; few variables in the final model must not mean shallow discovery. Bound the research by materiality and the requested depth, not an impossible promise to know everything.

## Reconstruct How The Advantage Was Built

When durability or a moat is load-bearing, a current snapshot is insufficient. Reconstruct the dated formation path that produced today's economics: the customer problem and initial constraint; consequential product, distribution, pricing, organization or capital-allocation choices; capabilities and relationships accumulated; failed paths or tradeoffs absorbed; and the resulting behavior in retention, price, cost, reinvestment or cash. Financial statements are outcomes to explain, not the explanation itself.

Test path dependence rather than writing a founder legend. Ask why a capable rival using the same visible business-model label has not reproduced the result. Identify the missing complements, sequencing, installed base, data/learning loop, trust, regulation, channel access, operating discipline, capital and elapsed time required for replication. Estimate or bound the cost and time to recreate the system where evidence permits. A strategy that is easy to describe can remain hard to execute; a complicated history can also leave no present advantage.

Use filings, contemporaneous company records and relevant customer, supplier and competitor evidence as the factual spine. Biographies, histories and interviews can reveal decisions, constraints and hypotheses, but are selected narratives: verify consequential episodes, stated motives and claimed causality against dated records and rival explanations. Avoid survivor bias and hindsight inevitability by examining feasible alternatives and comparable firms that made similar choices without the same outcome.

End the history at the present mechanism. State which accumulated asset or feedback still causes customers to choose, economics to improve or competitors to remain constrained; what is merely legacy reputation; and which observable change would show that the engine has weakened. Research hours, familiarity and narrative coherence do not raise conviction unless they resolve one of these links.

## Quality Signals And Their Competing Explanations

| Dimension | Concrete evidence to seek | What can imitate quality or hide weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Customer need and value | Actual workflow, avoided cost or added revenue, purchase frequency, usage, renewal, reasons for wins/losses and substitutes including doing nothing | Demonstrations, subsidized trials, engagement without willingness to pay, buyer/user mismatch |
| Demand durability | Comparable customer/product cohorts, repeat purchases, gross retention, volume versus price/mix, cancellations and backlog conversion | Acquisitions, channel loading, discounts, a temporary cycle, expansion revenue masking customer losses |
| Pricing and competition | Realized net prices after rebates, retention/volume after price changes, competitive bids, switching effort, measurable cost/service advantage | List-price increases, inflation, scarcity rents, lock-in without customer value; high margins alone do not identify a moat |
| Distribution and concentration | Acquisition channel economics, renewal versus new-customer effort, direct/customer access, dependence on a platform, distributor or large buyer | A cheap channel that another party controls, one anchor customer, pooled averages masking costly new cohorts |
| Unit economics and scalability | Contribution after delivery, support, incentives and acquisition; cohort payback, utilization, service quality and incremental operating costs | Gross margin excluding necessary costs, favorable mix, deferred support/maintenance, utilization at a cyclical peak |
| Cash and accounting quality | Earnings-to-cash bridge, receivables/collections, inventory, refunds, deferred revenue, cash taxes, maintenance capex and SBC/dilution | Customer prepayments, delayed supplier payments, factoring, capitalized costs, one-off releases or buybacks masking issuance |
| Reinvestment and growth runway | New versus mature unit returns, investment lag, capacity ramp, demand evidence, cannibalization and returns after full funding needs | Legacy average ROIC extrapolated to new projects, TAM mistaken for obtainable profitable demand, acquisition EPS accretion |
| Management and governance | Dated promises versus delivery, capital deployment outcomes, incentives, related parties, disclosure consistency and treatment of minority holders | Founder charisma/ownership, confident calls, selectively adjusted KPIs, narrative shifts without reconciliations |
| Resilience and failure modes | Supplier/platform dependency, replacement technology, debt maturity and covenants, fixed commitments, safety/regulatory obligations and customer harm | Recent calm or net cash hiding gross obligations; lack of disclosure is uncertainty, not proof of misconduct |

Choose signals for the economic model: software needs customer/cohort and service economics, marketplaces need participant outcomes and net take economics, retailers need repeat demand and store/inventory economics, capital-intensive operators need utilization and lifecycle returns. Financial institutions require funding, credit/underwriting and regulatory-capital analysis, not a forced industrial FCF/ROIC template. Do not apply one sector's threshold to another.

When longevity or survival supports a quality claim, distinguish continuity of the product/business, the organization and original common shareholders' economic interests. Trace a relevant adaptation through changed customer needs, management actions, capital consumed and retained per-share value; examine rescue financing, restructuring or ownership changes where material. Age and survival are leads for this inquiry, not a quality score. A business can persist after old equity is impaired, while a sale can end independence and still reward shareholders. Assess the demonstrated adaptation and its cost under today's conditions rather than inferring future resilience from years in existence or chart performance.

When growth depends on adjacent markets, test the proposed transfer: which customer needs and paid commitments pull the company outward, which product, channel or capabilities carry over, and which new delivery costs, competition and capital requirements arise? Distinguish repeatable demand across relevant customers from one customer's bespoke project, verbal interest or a large addressable-market estimate. Customer-led expansion can support demand while leaving replicable unit economics unresolved; assess both, including concentration and any harm to the core business. Use the existing growth assumptions and evidence record only when this expansion carries the thesis.

## When Technology May Reorganize The Value Chain

Use a dated strategic history only when it explains present capabilities, sunk commitments, dependence or bargaining power. Reconstruct the choices and feasible alternatives, not a hindsight story in which today's outcome was inevitable. Separate observed actions from claimed motives. A rival's vertical integration does not by itself prove it can acquire customers or operate more profitably without a distributor.

For disruption or disintermediation, map the functions before and after the change: which disappear, which remain, who performs and finances them, and who owns demand, assets, data, liability and pricing. Test both removal and continued value of the incumbent. Distribution may still improve demand density, utilization, geographic coverage, payments or exception handling; whether that benefit exceeds its fee is an empirical question, not a platform slogan.

Compare alternatives on matched unit economics and service scope. For a hypothetical autonomous-mobility case, separate vehicle sale price from manufacturing cost and fleet acquisition cost; include paid versus empty miles, utilization, asset life, funding, insurance, charging, cleaning, maintenance, remote assistance, customer acquisition and platform fees where material. Do not add overlapping cost categories. Ask at what utilization and net fare direct operation beats partnership, and which assumptions have evidence. Lower capex alone does not establish that idle capacity is economical.

Distinguish technical demonstration, authorized operating domain, permits, deployed fleet, active availability, paid trips and sustainable cash returns. A software-capable installed base is not automatically a commercially deployable fleet: ownership consent, hardware eligibility, reliability, insurance, local permissions and operating support may bind. Safety comparisons need matched exposure, geography, severity and reporting definitions; strong results in one domain do not imply universal regulatory clearance.

Show competing paths—direct integration, partnership or a mixed market—when each is plausible, with observable implications for customer acquisition, supplier dependence, utilization, take rate and capital intensity. Do not infer a rival's terminal business model from fundraising size, a product launch or a single quote. Current cash generation and future franchise durability may diverge; connect the threat to specific economics and time horizons rather than treating a share-price decline as proof of its cause.

## When Data Is Claimed As A Moat

For a thesis that depends on proprietary data or feedback, trace real customer demand -> usable observations -> trustworthy labels/feedback -> out-of-sample improvement -> customer economics -> cash retained by the company. Data volume, exclusivity and collection difficulty alone establish none of the later links.

Separate task realism from verification difficulty: a realistic workflow may have ambiguous outcomes, while an easy-to-score benchmark may miss customer value. Examine coverage, freshness, selection bias, lawful use/retention rights, expert adjudication and cleaning costs. Ask whether competitors can buy, synthesize or collect substitutes. Do not treat weak disclosure as proof the moat does not exist.

Seek incremental evidence under comparable models, compute, task mix and human assistance; isolate data gains from model upgrades or hidden manual service. Distinguish familiar-task coverage from transfer to new customers, periods or workflows. Connect measured gains to retention, realized pricing or delivery cost after acquisition, labeling, inference and support expenses. If public evidence cannot separate these effects, mark the data-moat claim unresolved rather than invent an ablation or assign an automatic valuation premium. This branch is not required for unrelated businesses.

## Make Quality Judgments Auditable

For each load-bearing quality claim, record the observation and original source/date, metric definition and population, history and relevant comparator, interpretation, strongest plausible rival, and the observation that would change the judgment. Reuse the existing evidence table; no new ledger is required. Separate the current quality level, improvement/deterioration and confidence in measurement. Do not invent a universal quality score or fixed metric threshold.

Use the latest four reported periods to identify changes, but test durability against relevant longer history, cycles, cohorts and comparable peers. Reconcile mix, acquisitions, accounting and denominator changes before inferring improvement. Strong net retention can coexist with weak customer retention; expanding margins can coexist with deteriorating service or underinvestment. Investigate these joint patterns rather than treating each favorable metric as independent confirmation.

For a material quality claim, explain which related indicators should move together, with what lag, if the proposed mechanism holds. Compare demand with collections and inventory; claimed operating leverage with necessary support/maintenance spending and service outcomes; AI usage with paid conversion and contribution after delivery costs; EPS with total earnings, cash generation and net shares. Select only the relationships relevant to the claim, not a mandatory matrix. Directional agreement is not independent causal proof, and a divergence is an investigation trigger, not a verdict of deterioration or misconduct. Resolve material divergences through the [underwriting investigation loop](underwriting-loop.md#investigate-material-anomalies), without loading the entire Full workflow for a narrow question.

Read accessible product/pricing/contract material and relevant customer, supplier and competitor disclosures alongside financial statements and calls. Public interviews, reviews and field research can expose mechanisms but may have selection, sponsorship and sample bias; disclose what was actually observed. Do not arrange contact, purchase research, pose as a customer or access nonpublic information without authorization. Investigate both positive and negative evidence with the same standard; an unanswered question or missing KPI remains an evidence gap.

Maintain distinctions among: customer love, pricing power, operational efficiency, durable excess capital returns and shareholder treatment. One can improve while another deteriorates. Fast growth is not automatically quality; low growth is not automatically failure. Temporary pricing power or a good product may fail to produce durable shareholder cash.

## Deliver Understanding, Not Labels

Synthesize: how the business makes money; what is genuinely strong and the concrete evidence; weaknesses and dependencies; what is changing; what remains unverified. Explain technical industry terms through their economic consequence. Avoid using “network effect,” “culture,” “recurring revenue” or “high ROIC” as self-explanatory verdicts.

For an investment request, carry these findings into independent operating assumptions, capital requirements, valuation and falsifiers. Do not charge twice for the same quality through both optimistic cash flows and an unexplained multiple premium. For a business-only request, this synthesis and the highest-value unresolved questions are a complete deliverable; no target price or non-consensus claim is required.
